# Tier 1 — prove correctness

[The roadmap](../ROADMAP.md) says how a step runs, what comes next, and which dependencies cross tiers. Each row below keeps its text as it was planned — Item, Today, Exit criterion, Doc, Depends on — and is cut into steps, each one workflow run.

These come before any new feature, with tier 0. Ruling 13 says the engine
agreeing with itself is the only kind of proof this repository will have,
which raises the bar on what the checks must be: answers computable in
closed form, bitstreams transcribed from the standards' own annexes,
published conformance data, and thousands of documents nobody here
authored. What this tier cannot contain is stated once: the four properties
that left with the oracles ([verification.md](../verification.md), "What does
not come back") do not return and are not rows (NG-04).

**What the closed rows of this tier and of the old refusals tier left
behind**, one paragraph each.

**Thirty-six password-refused files became measurements** (5–6 September
2026): [corpus/passwords.tsv](../../corpus/passwords.tsv) carries 34 rows, each
quoted to the upstream line that states it, two marked `derived` because no
upstream file names them, and one carrying a SASLprep-normalised form with
the gap that requires it named. Two files stay refused and neither is a
password: one misspells its key length as `/Wength 128`, and one is 91
bytes of a structural tool's own fuzzer output with `/O` and `/U` both
empty.

**`ROTATE_BUDGET` is 10 %**, and the measurement that raised it corrected
the one that prompted it: on pages the construct covers, a page of 11-point
text costs **8.77 %** and stays there as the page grows, so two percent was
out by a factor of four. Ten sits above that and below the tiling class at
22 %; it costs signal — 181 corpus failures become 15 — so the same
measurement is judged again at 3 % as `rotate-tight`, which the ratchet
holds and no run fails on. A page saturated with diagonal edge costs
23.5 %, the defect class's own range, so no budget separates those two
populations.

**Annex F numbering closed on 20 September 2026**, and reading the clause
corrected the row three times: parts 7, 8 and 9 are numbered sequentially
from 1 and the head after them; the head holds the catalogue, the
document-level objects and page one but also part 2 and the primary hint
stream, because the first-page cross-reference section is one subsection
and 7.5.4 makes a subsection a contiguous range; Annex F reserves no object
numbers; and F.3.6 gives the hint stream the last object number in the
file, which the structural tool's suite's `lin1.pdf` shows. The exit
criterion is `crates/tinker-pdf-cos/tests/linearized_numbering.rs` and
`validate/hints.rs`'s `annex_f_numbering`, which find every page of this
writer's output and of 31 linearized files in that suite — `badlin1.pdf`,
its deliberately damaged fixture, named as the one that does not. The flip
found the strict validator's `/E` rule inert on every conforming file; it
recomputes by reachability now, and over the suite's 45 linearized files
none reports `/E`.

**The codec rows closed, and their method is kept because it
generalises.** JPEG arithmetic coding (SOF9 and SOF10) closed on 20
September 2026 and JPX on 23 September, when `BYPASS` and `TERMALL` landed
and Table A.19 left the refusal list — `jpx_attribution.rs` finds the same
39 JPX files, 7 refusals and five reasons after the closure as before it,
none of them a coding capability; every JBIG2-bearing real document in
five corpora decodes but one, the viewer suite's `issue3371.pdf`, a stream
that stops inside a segment, which `Jbig2Refusal::is_malformed` calls damage
and `jbig2_attribution.rs` pins by name. The rule that generalises: *read
the annex list before asserting that a standard publishes no data.* T.88,
T.800 and T.81 each published what the roadmap had assumed did not exist —
T.800 Annex J.10's complete codestream with its packet extents and
intermediate coefficients, B.10.7.1's and B.10.7.2's worked header bit
sequences, T.81 Annex K.4.1's arithmetic-coder vector with its per-event
traces — and four levers came out of the searches: published intermediate
coefficients adjudicate what acts on coefficients (`jpx_annex_h.rs`);
published packet extents adjudicate what moves a packet (`jpx_annex_j.rs`,
`jpx_poc.rs`); B.10.7.2 makes what happens inside codeword segments visible
in the packet header (`code_block_styles.rs`); and a published header bit
sequence is the standard's bytes in and the standard's numbers out. A
specification whose text layer is garbled is still readable: render the
page with a face supplied (`tpdf render --fonts`) and read the image. And
having the specification is necessary and not sufficient — of 182 citations
six implementation specs drew from T.800, 37 were wrong, and a second
reading against the document is what caught them. Two limits are named
rather than hidden: `TERMALL` has no link whose expected output is
T.800's, so Table D.8 is transcribed and asserted as data; and the
arithmetic JPEG statistical model has nothing published to adjudicate it
(NG-07).

**The G4 encoder's exit criterion changed, and the change is the point.**
It asked for the encoder to be held to the T.4/T.6 coder the TIFF tests
carry — a coder this repository wrote, which is the failure ruling 13 and
the self-round-trip rule exist to name. It was held instead to ITU-T T.4
Tables 2, 3a, 3b and 4, read twice, all 195 run-length entries asserted in
`ccitt::tests::the_run_tables_are_itu_t_t_4_s_own`, and to T.88 Annex H.1's
segment 4, 26 bytes of MMR coding a picture the same annex publishes; the
JBIG2 generic-region encoder to the same annex's segment 11. No published
bitstream can pin a context numbering, because a coder's output is blind to
any bijection of it, so T.88's Figures 8 to 11 pin it instead.

**The nine reviewed goldens are this tier's remaining exit, and a person's
reading**: H-60, in [H.md](H.md). K-08 extends the mechanism to every
new rendered surface.

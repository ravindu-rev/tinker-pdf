## CD-01 — EPUB and HTML CSS: the thirty-seven known and unimplemented properties

**EPUB and HTML CSS: the thirty-seven known and unimplemented properties** — `animation`, `background-attachment`, `border-image`, `caption-side`, `clip`, `clip-path`, `cursor`, `empty-cells`, `filter`, `font`, `font-display`, `font-stretch`, `font-variant-numeric`, `grid` and its seven related properties (`grid-area`, `grid-column`, `grid-row`, `grid-template`, `grid-template-areas`, `grid-template-columns`, `grid-template-rows`), `justify-items`, `justify-self`, `list-style-image`, `mix-blend-mode`, `page`, `resize`, `speak`, `src`, `tab-size`, `text-emphasis`, `text-emphasis-style`, `text-overflow`, `transition`, `unicode-range`, `word-wrap`, `writing-mode` — and the counted remainders (repeating and conic gradients, translucent stops and hints, blurred shadows, 3D transforms, `opacity` as a true group, a second background layer, `reversed()`, bidi overrides and right-to-left tables, flex and multi-column), custom properties and `calc()`

## CD-19 — What the overflow font's code-32 move hid under `word-spacing`

**What the overflow font's code-32 move hid under `word-spacing`**

**Open cases.**

- Left (1): a Hebrew paragraph with no `dir` or `dir="ltr"` that ends a number and a stop (`<p>בראשית. אור ٦٠.</p>` at `word-spacing: 0.5em`), or a dir-less line whose left end is a closing bracket (`<p>שלום.<br/>בראשית (אלהים).</p>`), reads its stop first: ruling 14's comma tie-break, which asks for a strong right-to-left character at the line's left end, takes neither line.
- Left (3): a word space styled apart after a bold Greek, Cyrillic or Armenian word before a bare right-to-left gloss and a Latin tail (`αλφα <b>βήτα</b> ספר בית xyz.` at `word-spacing: 2em`) does not conserve: each half of the two-em stretch is past the half em within which `TextDevice` resumes a line.
- Left (4): lines K-11's reader reads from the right whatever the writer draws: a Latin gloss before a bracketed right-to-left word (`<p>abc def (كتاب) بيت</p>` at `word-spacing: 0.5em` or `12px`); table cells with no `dir` (`<td>abc —</td><td>(ספר) בית</td>` at `12px`, `25، <b>W</b>` in a cell, two right-to-left cells touching, read as one line whose left end is the second cell's `)`); a dir-less Hebrew line ending an ARABIC QUESTION MARK, ARABIC FULL STOP or SOF PASUQ after `(abc …)`; the Persian digits `٢٠١٠. ۴۵` in a letter-spaced, word-spaced and justified right-to-left Hebrew paragraph; and, with `bundled-fonts`, pointed Hebrew with two bracket pairs ending in the ARABIC COMMA, letter-spaced and justified.
- Left (5): a run that mixes the stops (`.!`, `!…`) opening a right-to-left line before a styled pair after a `<br/>`, with no spacing, does not conserve, nor does a dir-less Arabic paragraph whose inline `word-spacing: 1em` span cuts a word; the structure tree of a book with a `'.` at `word-spacing: 0.5em` does not read in order where the overflow font at `0 Tw` meets ruling 14's rule; and an Arabic line with `;` glued to a word, letter-spaced by `0.25em` and justified, does not read whole.
- Left (6): `25٪ <b>W</b> ١٢٣ … 2010،` in an Arabic paragraph turned by `rotate(180deg)` at `word-spacing: 1em` reads right in no drawing tried: `TextDevice` asks whether a glyph continues a line along the page's `x`, which a half turn runs the other way. - Left (7): with `bundled-fonts`, a word of a dir-less Hebrew paragraph of inline blocks squeezed by `scaleX(0.5)` (`W <span style="display: inline-block; transform: scaleX(0.5)">W <b>?!</b> W</span> W 25٪ W.` at `word-spacing: 0.6em`) is not found by search; its cause is not found yet.

- Left (9): with `bundled-fonts`, `W 1 234، <b>W</b> W.` in Hebrew, the number's halves a NO-BREAK SPACE apart, under `scaleX(2)` at `word-spacing: 0.6em`, reads its ARABIC COMMA apart (`עולם ،`); searching the line again as typed does not reach it, and its cause is not found yet.
- Left (10): a dir-less Arabic paragraph holding, twice, an inline block widened by `scaleX(2)` with a word and a styled `?!` in it, beside one squeezed by `scaleX(0.6)` to `0.8`, at `word-spacing: 0.5em` or `0.55em`, reads in logical order but draws glyphs over others, also under a quarter turn: `paint::visual_lines` orders each block's runs among the line's and leaves its box where layout set it, and its transform is applied about the box, so its glyphs land on its neighbours'.

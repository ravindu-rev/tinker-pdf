# Tier 0 — measure what is not measured

[The roadmap](../ROADMAP.md) says how a step runs, what comes next, and which dependencies cross tiers. Each row below keeps its text as it was planned — Item, Today, Exit criterion, Doc, Depends on — and is cut into steps, each one workflow run.

Each row here is an axis the field judges an engine on and this repository
has no number for, so a claim about it would be the kind of claim ruling 13
exists to prevent. Four rows closed in September 2026, and what closed them
is kept, one paragraph each, because those measurements are the reason the
rest of the roadmap may quote a number at all.

**The production corpus closed on 5–6 September 2026, and paid for itself
at once.** `corpus/corpora.lock` pins a thousand documents a crawler found
on the open web, spanning 462 distinct `/Producer` strings, and its first
run said fourteen of them could not be rendered inside a minute. That was
not the corpus and not the timeout: it was two loops in the rasteriser
indexing with a bounds check per pixel, which cost **9× on a page of a real
word-processor document** and which no fixture corpus had ever said a word
about. Fixing it closed the vectorisation row in the same measurement, with
every fingerprint unchanged on every target ([verification.md](../verification.md),
"The rasteriser's row loop"). What that corpus refuses is three files of a
thousand, and not one is a page this engine drew wrongly: one is genuinely
encrypted, and two are HTML pages the crawler saved under a `.pdf` name.

**The JBIG2 counts settled with it.** `MAX_JBIG2_SYMBOLS`,
`MAX_JBIG2_SYMBOL_PIXELS` and `MAX_JBIG2_TEXT_INSTANCES` published the word
"estimate" because the largest `SDNUMEXSYMS` anywhere in the corpus was
**11**, every one of them synthetic, so a cap calibrated on them would have
admitted anything. Over 118 JBIG2-bearing files including the production
corpus's, the largest is **2 478 exported symbols, 2 468 new ones and 4 440
text instances** — real OCR output, from documents scanned by people. The
caps stand at 100 000 symbols and 4 194 304 instances, which is 40 and 944
times what a real document has asked for.

**The symbol-pixel budget settled on 26 September 2026 and brought a
fourth row with it.** `MAX_JBIG2_SYMBOL_PIXELS` is a *pixel* budget and the
figures above are counts, and **neither of a symbol's dimensions is in any
segment header**, so `jbig2_census.rs`, which shares no code with the
decoder on purpose, could count symbols and could not measure one. It
decodes for that half now. The largest dictionary in five corpora spends
**1 568 118** pixels, which the cap clears by 42.8×. The same pass answered
the question the `jbig2` fuzz row had been left on — the largest symbol
*relative to its page*, which over 88 736 corpus symbols is **never more
than one** — and `MAX_JBIG2_SYMBOL_PAGE_MULTIPLE` is the bound chosen from
it, at 4.

**Speed got a ratchet on 19 September 2026, and the ratchet is not enough.**
`bench.yml` has a `workflow_dispatch` on `ubuntu-latest`; fifteen
dispatches of one unchanged revision measured that runner's own swing over
the seven operations — 22.61 % for "extract a page of text", 88.36 % for
"render text at 150 dpi" — and `crates/tinker-pdf/benches/baseline.json`
carries each operation's fastest figure and its own band above its own
swing; `cargo xtask baseline` holds that file, each `benches/<group>.rs` and the
weekly job's count for it to each other ([verification.md](../verification.md),
"Clocks: outside the suite, and not nowhere"). The old diagnosis of why the
weekly job never worked was half wrong, and the wrong half is the one worth
keeping: **a scheduled workflow runs the default branch**, which is `main`,
hundreds of commits behind `develop`, so the cron runs benchmarked a
months-old tree (H-01). And what the ratchet does not do: `ubuntu-latest`
is a fleet and not a machine — the fifteen runs fall into two groups, four
at 1.00 to 1.16 of the fastest and eleven at 1.30 to 1.36, on one runner
image, and the job records `/proc/cpuinfo`'s model name now so the next
measurement can attribute the split — and a band above that spread is
wide; the text renderer's is 135 %,
and a change that makes it half again slower passes in silence, where the
9× rasteriser regression of 5 September would not have. The swing has not
converged either (57.59 % over five runs, 72.71 % over nine, 88.36 % over
fifteen). The finer comparison is still criterion's own `--baseline`
between two revisions on one machine. **That is why this tier reopens**:
the owner made speed an axis with targets, and the instrument that exists
cannot see a 1.5× regression. The rows below build the instrument tier P is
held to, and the censuses no corpus run has fed.

## MS-01 — Counted-work guards on every pull request and every target

**Counted-work guards on every pull request and every target.** A first-party `Work` tally — plain counters, no clock, no `unsafe` — threaded through the operation contexts the work budgets already use, and surfaced as an opt-in statistics value on the facade (`RenderOptions::stats` → `Bitmap::stats()`, the same for text, save, merge and decode) so the guards test the public surface (ruling 11)

**Today.** Counted-work tests exist one at a time, each written after a quadratic was found (`moving_many_children_is_linear`, `a_hostile_run_of_regional_indicators_is_linear`, `a_line_of_nothing_but_marks_is_linear_and_whole`); no shared counter type; `bounds_ledger.rs` bans `Instant::now` in tests; nothing guards page lookup, per-glyph font resolution, decode counts, bytes inflated or cache hits

**Exit criterion.** `cargo test` on linux, windows, macos and `wasm32-wasip1` runs the guard suite — A1 `page(i)` visits ≤ depth × fan-out nodes and every page of a generated 10 000-page document ≤ 3 × 10 000 in total; A2 work(4 000 pages) ≤ 4.4 × work(1 000) for text, render-all at 36 dpi, rewrite, rotate, merge, split, subset, redaction and validation; A3 font dictionaries resolved per page ≤ distinct `/Font` entries used; A4 glyph masks rasterised ≤ distinct (font, glyph, scale, phase) keys, hit ratio ≥ 98 % on a 5 600-glyph page; A5 inflate calls per stream ≤ 1 per operation; A6 merge deduplication decompresses nothing unless encoded length and encoded hash collide; A7 IDCT output ≤ 4 × drawn pixels for a quarter-scale draw, JPX levels decoded ≤ levels needed; A8 whole-image scratch on the PNG path ≤ 1 × the image; A9 band- and page-parallel renders byte-identical to serial at 2, 3, 4 and 7 threads; A10 allocations O(1) per page. Each guard lands with its P row and is shown failing against the code before the fix, the failing run named in the commit

## MS-02 — A paired base-versus-head wall-clock gate on every pull request

**A paired base-versus-head wall-clock gate on every pull request.** Base and head built in one job on one machine, the suite run interleaved ten rounds, each operation judged by the median paired ratio and a sign test (fails when the ratio exceeds 1 + band and at least 8 of 10 pairs are slower)

**Today.** `bench.yml` is weekly plus dispatch and never on a pull request; its cron runs the default branch, hundreds of commits behind `develop` (`.github/workflows/bench.yml:29-40`); the text renderer's 135 % band lets a 1.5× regression pass (`verification.md:1779-1784`)

**Exit criterion.** `cargo xtask bench-check --paired BASE HEAD` reads criterion's `estimates.json`/`sample.json` for both (no second timing), with unit tests including an injected 10 % slowdown that it catches and an A/A pair it passes; a pull-request job runs it in ≤ 15 minutes; each operation's band = worst A/A paired spread over 15 dispatches × 1.5, rounded up to 5 %, floor 5 %, recorded in `verification.md`; the same job builds the MS-04 suite a second time against `61adcde`, the planning baseline, and holds each operation's paired ratio to its committed factor floor, its calibrated targets against the calibration operation run in the job, and its relations (tier P's Gate column)
